# Comparison of Ideal vs Real Diode
In this project, a diode circuit was analyzed in LTspice using two models: an ideal diode and a real 1N4148 diode. A DC sweep from 0 V to 5 V was performed for both cases, with a series resistor of 1 kΩ.
For the ideal diode, current begins flowing immediately at 0 V. The I–V characteristic is linear, limited only by the series resistor, and there is no voltage drop across the diode. This behavior reflects a perfect switch that turns on instantly with zero forward voltage.
In contrast, the 1N4148 diode shows a forward voltage threshold of approximately 0.6–0.7 V. Below this voltage, almost no current flows. Once the threshold is reached, the current rises rapidly in an exponential manner, reflecting the real diode’s I–V behavior. This demonstrates the turn-on voltage and non-linear conduction of a practical diode.
Ideal diode models are used in simulations because they simplify analysis, reduce computational effort, and help understand general circuit behavior without worrying about precise voltage drops. They are particularly useful for initial design and logic-level circuit analysis. However, ideal models become inaccurate when precise voltage levels, power dissipation, or high-speed switching are important. Real diode models provide more accurate predictions in these cases, as seen in the 1N4148 simulation.
The comparison between ideal and real diode simulations highlights how forward voltage, current behavior, and conduction characteristics differ, and why real diodes must be considered in practical circuit design.
